  I have inherited shares


  do I pay any taxes
				
		<p>If you have inherited shares, you won't have to pay tax at the time of inheriting the shares.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:heading --></p>
<h2><strong>EXEMPTION</strong></h2>
<p><!-- /wp:heading --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>If the shares were given to you by your <strong>spouse or a civil partner</strong> who live in UK permanently during their life time you won't have to pay any inheritance tax. This will be considered a gift and any gift made to a spouse or a civil partner are tax exempt.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>If the shares were given to you by your parents, family or friends during their life time and their value was within the annual exemption amount of <strong>£3000</strong>, (any unused annual exemption can also carried forward to next year) then no tax will need to be paid on them even if they die within 7 years of giving the gift.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>If the shares were given to you by any of your family member or friends during their lifetime of any value and they didn't die within the <strong>7 years of giving the shares</strong> then no tax will need to be paid on receiving the shares.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:heading --></p>
<h2><strong>INHERITANCE TAX:</strong></h2>
<p><!-- /wp:heading --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Inheritance tax will need to be paid if the shares given to you by a member of the family or a friend died within 7 years of giving the shares to you as a gift.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Gifts made between 3 to 7 years before dying are taxed on a `sliding scale'. Inheritance tax will be paid at 40 % on all the gifts given within 3 years before death.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:heading --></p>
<h2><strong>INCOME TAX:</strong></h2>
<p><!-- /wp:heading --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>You will have to pay income tax on any dividends that you will receive from the inherited shares.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p><strong>Personal allowance:</strong> <br />You are given a personal allowance each tax year. For the current tax year which is from 6 April 2020 to 5 April 2021 the personal allowance is £12, 500. You will only pay income tax on any income received above this amount.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p><strong>Dividend allowance:</strong> <br />Each year you are given a dividend allowance as well. The dividend allowance for the tax year 2020/2021 is £2000. This means that you can get dividend for up to this amount without paying any tax and you don't even have to tell HMRC about it.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>You will have to pay tax on any dividend above this amount depending on which tax band you fall into.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:heading --></p>
<h2><strong>CAPITAL GAINS TAX:</strong></h2>
<p><!-- /wp:heading --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>You will have to pay <strong>Capital Gains Tax</strong> when you make a profit on selling the shares. Tax you pay on your gains will depend on which income band you fall into. Basic rate tax payer will pay 10% and higher and additional rate tax payer will pay 20 % CGT.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:heading {"level":3} --></p>
<h3><strong>HOW TO WORK OUT CGT:</strong></h3>
<p><!-- /wp:heading --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>When you sell your shares you need to calculate your CGT. This is the difference between the selling price and the price that you paid at the time of purchase. In this case when you have inherited the shares the CGT will be the difference between the `Selling price and the market value'. If you get a gain you need to pay CGT. However, there are different reliefs available which can help you delay the payment of CGT. To find out about such reliefs click <strong><a href="https://www.gov.uk/tax-sell-shares/relief" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">here</a>.</strong></p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
