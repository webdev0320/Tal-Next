					<h2 id="vat-threshold" style="font-size:28px; font-weight:400; color:#1d3c45;">
    <strong>UK VAT registration threshold 2025
</strong>
</h2>
    <p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8; padding-left:20px;">
●   You must register if your VAT taxable turnover is over £90,000.
<br>
●   If your turnover is less than this sum, you are free to voluntarily register.
<br>
●   Registration applies to <strong>goods, services, and some business transactions.</strong>
</p>
    <h3 id="vat-responsibilities" style="font-size:20px; font-weight:400; color:#1d3c45; padding-left:20px;">
    <strong>Your Responsibilities as a VAT-Registered Business
</strong>
</h3>
<p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8;padding-left:20px;">
      Once registered, you must:
    </p>
        <p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8; padding-left:20px;">
●   The prices of your goods and services include VAT at the correct rate.
<br>
●   Maintain track of VAT paid on purchases made for business.
<br>
●   Calculating VAT on goods imported into the UK
<br>
●   Typically, every three months, submit <strong>VAT returns to HMRC.</strong>
<br>
●   Pay any VAT you owe to HMRC.
</p>
        <p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8; padding-left:20px;">
      If you charged more VAT than you actually paid, you either pay the difference to HMRC or receive a <strong>VAT refunds.</strong>
    </p>
					<h2 id="vat-charged" style="font-size:28px; font-weight:400; color:#1d3c45;">
    <strong>VAT in the UK: What Is It Charged On?
</strong>
</h2>
    <p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8;">
      VAT is charged on an additional range of businesses, including:
</p>
    <p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8; padding-left:30px;">
●   Products and services
<br>
●   Hiring or lending goods
<br>
●   Selling company assets
<br>
●   Commission payments
<br>
●   Items sold to staff (e.g. meals)
<br>
●   Personal use of business goods
<br>
●   Bartering, part-exchange, and gifts
</p>
<p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8;">
       These are called <strong>“taxable supplies”.</strong>
    </p>
    <p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8;">
       Note: <strong>Charities may have different rules.</strong>
    </p>
    <h3 id="vat-not-charged" style="font-size:20px; font-weight:400; color:#1d3c45; padding-left:20px;">
    <strong>When VAT Is Not Charged
</strong>
</h3>
<p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8;padding-left:20px;">
      VAT is <strong>not charged on:</strong>
    </p>
        <p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8; padding-left:30px;">
●   Items that are exempt (e.g., some education and healthcare)
<br>
●   Goods and services that are outside the scope (VAT rules are not affected)
</p>				
					<h2 id="vat-rates" style="font-size:28px; font-weight:400; color:#1d3c45;">
    <strong>How Much <a href="https://www.taxaccolega.co.uk/blog/vat-accountants-in-london-how-taxaccolega-helps-businesses-stay-compliant-and-save-money/" style="color:#d2601a; font-weight:700; text-decoration:underline;">
VAT </a>  Should UK Businesses Charge?
</strong>
</h2>
    <p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8;">
      Depending on the good or service, there are three different VAT rates:
</p>
    <p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8; padding-left:30px;">
<strong>1</strong>   Standard rate of 20% for most trade and services
<br>
<strong>2</strong>   Decreased rate (5%): Some energy-efficient products, car seats for kids, etc.
<br>
<strong>3</strong>   The zero rate (0%) consists of:
<br>
●   Books and newspapers
<br>
●   Children’s clothes and shoes
<br>
●   Motorcycle helmets
<br>
●   Most exports outside the UK or EU
</p>
    <h3 id="vat-exports-eu" style="font-size:20px; font-weight:400; color:#1d3c45; padding-left:20px;">
    <strong>VAT on Exports and EU Transactions
</strong>
</h3>
<p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8; padding-left:30px;">
●   Exports outside the UK are usually zero-rated.
<br>
●   Supplies from Northern Ireland to EU VAT-registered businesses may be zero-rated if the EU VAT number is valid.
</p>				
					<h2 id="vat-schemes" style="font-size:28px; font-weight:400; color:#1d3c45;">
    <strong>UK VAT Schemes for Simplifying VAT Accounting
</strong>
</h2>
    <p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8;">
      VAT schemes help certain businesses simplify how they calculate and report VAT.
      </p>
       <p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8;">
      They are optional and don’t affect the VAT rate you charge.
      </p>
    <p style="font-size:20px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8;">
      <strong>●  VAT Flat Rate Scheme
</strong>
      </p>
<p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8; padding-left:30px;">
●   Perfect for small companies with taxable turnovers under £150,000 (VAT excluded).
<br>
●   Depending on your industry, pay a set percentage of your turnover.
</p>
<p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8;">
      With a few exceptions, it is not possible to claim VAT on your purchase.
      </p>
    <p style="font-size:20px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8;">
      <strong>●  Annual Accounting Scheme
</strong>
      </p>
<p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8; padding-left:30px;">
●   Fit for companies with less than £1.35 million in revenue.
<br>
●   Instead of filing four VAT returns annually, submit just one.
<br>
●   Throughout the year, make advance payments.
</p>
<p style="font-size:20px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8;">
      <strong>●  Cash Accounting Scheme
</strong>
      </p>
<p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8; padding-left:30px;">
●   Additionally, for companies with annual revenue under £1.35 million.
<br>
●   Only pay VAT to HMRC when you receive payment from your clients, not when you send them an invoice.
<br>
●   Beneficial for cash flow management.
</p>
<p style="font-size:20px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8;">
      <strong>●  Retail Schemes and VAT Margin</strong>
      </p>
<p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8; padding-left:30px;">
●   <strong>VAT Margin Scheme:</strong> Only the profit margin, not the entire price, is subject to VAT when selling used goods.
<br>
●   <strong>VAT Retail Schemes:</strong> These are made to help retailers figure out VAT in bulk as opposed to on a per-transaction basis.
</p>
					<h2 id="vat-updates-2025" style="font-size:28px; font-weight:400; color:#1d3c45;">
    <strong>Recent VAT Updates 2025 Businesses Must Know
</strong>
</h2>
<p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8;padding-left:20px;">
      VAT Registration Threshold Increased
    </p>
    <p style="font-size:16px; color:#000; text-align:justify; line-height:1.8; padding-left:20px;">
●   As of 1 April 2024, the new limit is up to £90,000.
<br>
●   Impact: No smaller businesses need to register for VAT.
<br>
●   Previous threshold: £85,000.
</p>
    <h3 id="private-school-vat" style="font-size:20px; font-weight:400; color:#1d3c45; padding-left:20px;">
    <strong>VAT on Private School Fees Introduced

</h3>

●   Effective 1 January 2025
<br>
●   20% VAT on private school fees
<br>
●   It is estimated to raise £1.5 billion for the state education system.
</p>
