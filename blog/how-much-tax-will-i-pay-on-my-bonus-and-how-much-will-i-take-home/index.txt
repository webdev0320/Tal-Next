  How much tax will I pay on my bonus and 


  how much will I take home?
				
		<figure></figure>
<p><!-- /wp:image --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>The bonus you will get is considered your earnings so you will be taxed in the same way as your income.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>You will pay <a href="https://www.taxaccolega.co.uk/taxation/personal-income-tax-professionals-and-chartered-accountants/"><strong>income tax</strong></a> and <a href="https://www.taxaccolega.co.uk/blog/national-insurance-how-much-do-i-have-to-pay/"><strong>National insurance</strong> </a>on your bonus in addition to that any student loans will also be deducted.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Look at this example below:</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Mr J has an annual income of <strong>£100 000</strong> (which is his base salary) and he received a bonus of <strong>£10 000</strong> in a particular year. How much tax and NI will he pay?</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p><strong>Income Tax on base salary</strong></p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:table --></p>

<td>(£ 12 570)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Taxable income </td>
<td> £ 87 430</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Tax payable at (40%)</td>
<td>£ 34 972</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
</figure>
<p><!-- /wp:table --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>As you can see above Mr J as he is earning <strong>£100 000</strong> he is a higher rate taxpayer and therefore the bonus he will earn will also be taxed at <strong>40%</strong></p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>

<td>£9675</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
</figure>
<p><!-- /wp:table --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>If we add Mr J`s bonus for the year to the base salary the total income for the year will cross the <strong>£100 000</strong> threshold. This means that there will be a reduction in the personal allowance,</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>The <strong>HMRC</strong> rule is that your personal allowance goes down by <strong>£1</strong> for every <strong>£2</strong> that your <a href="https://www.gov.uk/guidance/adjusted-net-income" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"><strong>adjusted net income</strong> </a>is above <strong>£100 000.</strong> This means your allowance is 0 if your income is <strong>£125 140</strong> or above.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>In the above example Mr J will have to pay an extra income tax on £5000 because of the reduction in his personal allowance due to his income being <strong>£10 000</strong> above <strong>£100 000.</strong> As a result he will be paying extra tax of <strong>£2000 (40% of 5000)</strong></p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Look at the summary of the taxes you will be paying on your bonus:</p>

<p><!-- /wp:table --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>If you get a bonus of <strong>£10 000</strong> or more and you are a higher rate tax payer you will be bringing home only <strong>£3 675</strong> as cash, paying more than half of your hard work to the treasury.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>However, if you are a basic tax rate payer <strong>(earning between £12 571 to £50 270) </strong>and after getting the bonus you stay in the same band you will be paying tax on the bonus at 20% and you will be able to use your personal allowance fully.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<h3><strong>If you are Basic Tax rate Payer</strong></h3>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>For example if you are a basic tax rate payer earning <strong>£40 000</strong> and you get a bonus of <strong>£10 000</strong> in a particular year you will pay income tax of <strong>£2000</strong> on the bonus and NI will be <strong>£1350 £3350</strong> so your take home bonus will be <strong>£6650 </strong>.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<h2><strong>How to save taxes on your bonus?</strong></h2>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>So Tax is a complex topic and how much you pay depends on your individual circumstances. One way to save taxes on your bonus is to put it in your <a href="https://www.taxaccolega.co.uk/resources/tax-rates-and-allowances/pensions"><strong>pension</strong></a> pot. Although you will be saving money as you won't have to pay taxes, you will not be getting any cash in hand until you reach your retirement age.</p>
