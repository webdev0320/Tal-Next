  How to calculate and Report them?


  Profits and Losses from UK Rental 
				
		<p><!-- wp:image --></p>
<figure></figure>
<p><!-- /wp:image --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>If you have rented a property in the UK, you should declare it to HMRC and pay tax on your profits.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>If you rent a property abroad and you are a UK resident you should be paying tax on it as well but the profits and losses on the rental income from overseas property should be done separately.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:heading --></p>
<h2><strong>WHAT TO DO IF I HAVE PROFITS?</strong></h2>
<p><!-- /wp:heading --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>If you have profited from your rental income, you should report it to HMRC in the <strong><a href="https://www.taxaccolega.co.uk/news/blog/archive/article/2020/august/as-a-landlord-how-will-i-be-taxed-on-the-rental-income-and-what-tax-deadlines-should-i-be-aware-of">self-assessment tax return</a></strong>. You should register yourself by 5 Oct following the tax year you had rental income and pay tax on it.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:heading --></p>
<h2><strong>WHAT TO DO IF I AM HAVING A LOSS ON MY RENTAL PROPERTY?</strong></h2>
<p><!-- /wp:heading --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>If you rent a property and it is making losses you are not required to declare it to HMRC unless you are already required to fill in the self-assessment tax return for some other reason.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p> If you are self-employed and you are already required to fill in the self-assessment tax return because you might be self-employed and running your own business then you should declare your income from all the sources even if it making a loss. However, it should be noted that the loss from the property business cannot be offset against the gain from any other business. It can only be offset either against the gains from the property business in the same tax year or carried forward against the future gains from the same property business.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>It is therefore advised that even if the property is making a loss it should be declared to HMRC so that the losses can be automatically carried forward and the loss figure is not challenged by HMRC.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>For example, if you have 2 rental properties one is making a profit and the other one is making a loss you should declare both to the HMRC.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:heading --></p>
<h2><strong>CALCULATING PROFIT</strong></h2>
<p><!-- /wp:heading --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>It is very important to know how the profit figure is calculated.</p>

<p><!-- /wp:table --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>If the 'overall figure' from your property business is a profit you will pay tax on it as in the example above and you will be paying tax on it.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>The tax you pay will depend on the tax rate band that you fall into.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>When you have income from other sources for example from employment, self-employment, or any other investment you have to add them all together.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Each year every individual is given a free allowance of £12, 500. Up to this amount an individual does not have to pay any income tax. Tax is paid at the rate of basic rate 20% (£12, 501- 50 000), higher rate 40% (£50 001- £ 150 000), Additional rate of 45% (over £150, 000). </p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>The first £1000 of your income from your rental property is tax-free this is called property allowance. You will pay tax on the income above this.</p>
