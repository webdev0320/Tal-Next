  VAT Implications


  Guide for UK Online Store Owners
				
		<p dir="ltr" style="line-height: 1.38; margin-top: 0pt; margin-bottom: 11pt;">If you are thinking of starting a business online where you will be selling your product through your e store you will have to register your business as usual.</p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height: 1.38; margin-top: 0pt; margin-bottom: 11pt;">You will also have to comply with other legal obligations such as registering yourself for VAT if you cross the threshold of £90,000.If you are supplying <strong><a href="https://www.taxaccolega.co.uk/taxation/vat-tax-returns-accountant/">VAT</a></strong> able supplies and you are not VAT registered you will have to bear heavy penalties.</p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height: 1.38; margin-top: 0pt; margin-bottom: 11pt;">Being online means that you will be selling products in UK as well as internationally. So when and how you will have to register for VAT?</p><h2 dir="ltr" style="line-height: 1.32; margin-top: 21pt; margin-bottom: 11pt;"><strong>WHEN SHOULD I REGISTER FOR VAT?</strong></h2><p dir="ltr" style="line-height: 1.38; margin-top: 0pt; margin-bottom: 11pt;">There are different thresholds for online businesses depending on where the business is registered and from where the business is being operated, who the customers are and what the product is which is being sold.</p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height: 1.38; margin-top: 0pt; margin-bottom: 11pt;">It is a very simple concept, if you are operating your business from UK you will have to register for UK VAT. Just make sure that you charge the right rate of VAT.</p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height: 1.38; margin-top: 0pt; margin-bottom: 11pt;">If you have an online store you should be aware of the terms such as :</p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height: 1.38; margin-top: 0pt; margin-bottom: 11pt;" role="presentation"> ● Distance Selling: Distance selling means that you are outside UK but in the EU country and you are selling your product to a customer in UK who are not VAT register either because they are selling product which is exempt from VAT or they are individuals or small businesses who are not required to register themselves for VAT.</p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height: 1.38; margin-top: 0pt; margin-bottom: 11pt;">If the annual sales in distance selling exceeds £70,000 the seller needs to register for UK VAT and charge VAT at the UK rate.</p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height: 1.38; margin-top: 0pt; margin-bottom: 11pt;" role="presentation"> ● Online Market place: If you are selling goods online, you might be operating in an online marketplace. Online marketplace is a website through which anyone can sell the goods. It does not matter if the online market place is established in UK or not, if you have access to the online marketplace or its contents you will be responsible for the online sellers unpaid VAT. And therefore you will have to make sure that the sellers are VAT registered if cross the threshold.</p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height: 1.38; margin-top: 0pt; margin-bottom: 11pt;">You don't have to register for VAT if you supply VAT exempt supplies such as equipment for disabled but you will be registering for VAT if you sell VAT exempt items but you are buying items of more than £90,000 from EU VAT registered suppliers.</p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height: 1.38; margin-top: 0pt; margin-bottom: 11pt;">It is very important that you apply for VAT on time, you should register for VAT as soon as you think that your Vatable supplies will exceed the threshold of £90,000 in the next 12 months. Otherwise you won't be able to charge VAT from your buyers as you won't have your VAT number, however, you will be paying VAT to HMRC for this period.</p><h2 dir="ltr" style="line-height: 1.32; margin-top: 21pt; margin-bottom: 11pt;">HOW SHOULD I REGISTER FOR VAT?</h2><p dir="ltr" style="line-height: 1.38; margin-top: 0pt; margin-bottom: 11pt;">Once you have crossed the threshold, you will have to register by post. By filling the form VAT1A if you are an EU business and distance selling the goods to UK you will have to fill in the form VAT 1A and post it to <strong><a href="https://www.taxaccolega.co.uk/blog/hmrc-penalties-investigations/">HMRC</a></strong>. Within 30 days of applying for VAT registration you will get a VAT registration certificate. It will have your VAT number, deadline of your VAT return and payment, and the effective date of VAT registration.</p><h2 dir="ltr" style="line-height: 1.32; margin-top: 21pt; margin-bottom: 11pt;">PENALTIES</h2><p dir="ltr" style="line-height: 1.38; margin-top: 0pt; margin-bottom: 11pt;">HMRC defines some serious penalties if you default meaning if you are not able to submit the VAT return on time, or if you made a mistake in your return and you do not correct it within 30 days.</p><h2 dir="ltr" style="line-height: 1.32; margin-top: 21pt; margin-bottom: 11pt;">HOW CAN WE HELP?</h2><p dir="ltr" style="line-height: 1.38; margin-top: 0pt; margin-bottom: 11pt;">We at Taxaccolega can help you register your business for VAT, prepare and submit your returns on time. We make sure that all the deadlines are met.
