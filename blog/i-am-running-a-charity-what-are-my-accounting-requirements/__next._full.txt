  I am running a Charity -


  What are my accounting requirements?
				
		<figure></figure>
<p><!-- /wp:image --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p><strong>Do I need to prepare my accounts?</strong></p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>All <strong><a href="https://www.taxaccolega.co.uk/blog/setting-up-a-charity-6-things-you-need-to-know/">charities</a></strong> ( whether they are registered with the commission or not)should prepare annual accounts and should be made available when requested.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>The way you prepare your accounts depends on the type of your charity and also the size of your charity.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>For example if your charity is also a company and it earns upto £25 000 per year you should prepare annual accrual accounts.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>The accrual accounts include information like</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:list --></p>
<ul><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>       ● How did your <strong><a href="https://www.taxaccolega.co.uk/industries/charity/">charity</a></strong> gets its money</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>       ● How your charity gets its money</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>       ● Money in your charity bank account</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>       ● Assets</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>       ● Liabilities '</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>       ● Any other information such as related party transaction</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<p><!-- /wp:list --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p><strong>Will I have to get my accounts audited?</strong></p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>You will have to get your accounts examined independently either by an independent examiner or by an auditor if your charity is also a company and earns over £25 000 to £50 000.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>You can choose how you want to get it examined, either by an independent examiner ( which is less thorough) or by the auditor. However, if your governing document states that your accounts need to be audited then you do require an auditor.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Look at this table below which summarizes the accounting requirements according to the type and size of the charity.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p><strong>If your charity is a Company</strong></p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>A charitable company will have to be limited by guarantee rather than shares. Your charity is considered a company if it is registered with the companies house.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>If your charity is also a company, what will be your accounting liabilities with different incomes:</p>

<p>You must submit your annual return within 10 months of the end of the financial year.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p><strong>If your charity is not a company</strong></p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>If your charity is not a company and the income is below £ 25 000 you can prepare either receipts and payments or accrual accounts.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Between £25000 to £ 250 000 you still have the option in preparing your accounts but have to get them checked.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Above £250 000 you have to prepare accrual accounts and also have them checked.</p>
